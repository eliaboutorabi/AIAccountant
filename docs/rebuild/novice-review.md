# Novice comprehension and prerequisite review

Prepared September 28, 2026. Scope: the approved rebuild specification and the introductory edition's instructional data/rendering. This is an editorial review by an AI reviewer, not an observed learner pilot. The implementation must be reviewed again as it is authored.

## Judgment and review standard

The proposed curriculum has an appropriate adult ambition. The original instruction often gives correct definitions but omits the intermediate reasoning that would let someone use them. More topics or longer paragraphs alone will not repair that. A learner needs a concrete situation, the mechanism connecting its parts, a worked example, a partially supported attempt, and a changed case.

Assume an intelligent adult who can read a table and perform basic arithmetic, but may not remember accounting vocabulary, percentages, elementary statistics, or spreadsheet syntax. Do not assume programming, matrix algebra, calculus, a paid AI account, Microsoft products, or a particular national accounting qualification. Offer a concise prerequisite recap at the relevant moment; do not make a learner leave the current argument repeatedly to reconstruct its foundations.

The target is accurate reasoning in a finance conversation: identifying the question, describing how a system produces its result, testing whether the result supports a decision, and stating the next action. Precise limitations are part of useful reasoning, but the course must teach positive mechanisms as thoroughly as it teaches warnings.

## How to teach the difficult mechanisms without equation manipulation

### Training: show a direction, an amount, and a result

Use an input expressed in hundreds of invoices so the arithmetic is inspectable. Suppose a model predicts handling cost as a fixed $20 plus $10 for each hundred invoices. At two hundred invoices it predicts $40, while the observed cost is $50. Keep the fixed amount unchanged for one demonstration. Increasing the variable amount by $1 changes the prediction to $42 and reduces this case's absolute error from $10 to $8. That establishes a local direction. Then show other rows: the same move can help one case and hurt another, so training uses an aggregate objective.

Only after this trace introduce “gradient” as local sensitivity and “learning rate” as the size of an update based on that sensitivity. Show a real computed aggregate update separately. Do not pretend the one-row $1 experiment is the whole algorithm. Backpropagation can then be introduced as an efficient way to work those local sensitivities backwards through connected calculations, with the optimizer using them to change parameters. Neither is a second intelligence grading the first model.

### Attention: use a full row rather than an isolated metaphor

Select the token in a short sentence whose representation is being updated. Show its starting list of numbers and the comparison with each available position. Explain that learned transformations produce queries, keys, and values from representations; the model does not retrieve a human-written list of questions or dictionary definitions. Show a single row of weights summing to one and the value mixture those weights produce. Mask future positions visibly before normalizing. Then add the residual path and feed-forward transformation, explaining that attention is one stage inside a block.

Use the same names and colors in the prose, 3D view, and accessible table. Keep learned parameter values visually distinct from attention weights recomputed for this sentence. A later prompt should reuse the same parameters but produce a different attention row. The resulting representation still feeds later computation; it is not yet the next word or a truth judgment.

### Join multiplication: let the learner see the repeated money

One invoice is $120 with two invoice lines ($80 and $40) and two payments ($50 and $70). Joining lines directly to payments on invoice ID produces four rows. The line amounts sum to $240 and the payment amounts to $240; the invoice header amount repeated on all rows sums to $480. Explain why each pair was created before offering the repair. Aggregate each child table to the required invoice grain first, or preserve separate related facts and define measures correctly. Matching grand totals after a mistaken join can be doubly wrong.

### Evidence: separate arithmetic contribution from causal explanation

In the margin case, show the bridge from budget gross profit to actual gross profit. Every movement reconciles, but this does not reveal why freight rates or sales volume changed. Ask the learner to make two lists: facts the supplied documents establish and additional evidence needed for a specific decision. The teaching should make clear that “unknown” can be followed by a precise next action. It is not a reason to discard the useful analysis already supported.

### Engineering: make state concrete before naming infrastructure

A trace should have columns for step, requested operation, validated arguments, actual result, and stored state. Interrupt after a calculation has completed but before a review item is created. Ask what the harness needs to remember to resume correctly. Introduce checkpoints, durable state, and idempotency through that trace, then show their names. Reading a JSON example is enough for the core; authoring a production service is an extension.

## Content acceptance gates for every module

Review each module against these gates after implementation. “Present somewhere in the course” is insufficient when the concept is needed earlier.

1. **A complete conceptual path.** The introduction states a concrete problem and the decision to make. Each new essential term is explained before it is used to explain another term. At least one worked example includes inputs, intermediate reasoning, result, interpretation, and limitation.
2. **No unexplained jump in tools.** Before a learner enters a formula, reads JSON, changes a training control, or executes a starter, show one annotated example and the expected effect. Specify what to do when a field is blank, input is invalid, or a run fails.
3. **Accounting support at the point of need.** State currency, units, period, perspective, and sign convention. A refresher answers the exact question needed by the task. It includes a miniature example when a definition alone is inadequate.
4. **Guidance fades.** First demonstrate a complete case, then ask the learner to finish a partially worked case, then offer a changed case without procedural prompts. Do not label copying the demonstrated numbers as transfer.
5. **The experiment teaches a mechanism.** Ask for a prediction; expose the relevant state; change one factor; interpret the result; ask for a different-case implication. Controls must have semantic labels and reset behavior. A decorative animation does not satisfy this gate.
6. **Assessment distinguishes understanding from recognition.** At least one check requires a calculation, trace, source judgment, counterexample, or explanation. Plausible alternatives represent specific misconceptions. Feedback explains why an answer follows and how a condition change might alter it.
7. **The written work is finishable.** Supply the records, schema, prompt, expected output structure, rubric, worked model answer, and a minimum adequate answer. “Build a pipeline” is not a usable novice assignment without a starting point and bounded task.
8. **The module reconnects.** It explicitly recalls one prior concept where relevant and identifies the artifact or idea used later. The link describes the reason to revisit it, not merely “see previous chapter.”
9. **Honest progress.** Reading, attempt, automatically checked result, and self-assessed reasoning remain distinguishable. Revealing a solution does not award unseen performance. A failed run cannot be counted as an observed successful experiment.
10. **Equivalent access.** Keyboard/touch users can reach definitions and operate experiments; information is available without hover, color, animation, 3D, WebGPU, or a paid product. Meaningful visual order and text remain synchronized.

Suggested editorial disposition: a missing prerequisite or unfinishable core task is a blocker; a misleading example, unsupported numerical claim, or mislabeled simulation is a blocker; small wording or optional enrichment gaps can be repaired without changing module status.

## Technical-word definitions: useful support, not hidden instruction

Give the first essential use a short in-sentence definition. The linked definition can then add a fuller explanation, a finance example, a commonly confused term, and the teaching-module link. For example: “A feature is an input available when a prediction is made. Agreed payment terms can be a feature; a future settlement date cannot be used for an issue-date forecast. See M02.”

Prefer one consistently styled term button per conceptual passage rather than marking every repetition. It should open on keyboard focus/activation and touch as well as pointer hover, remain available long enough to read and follow its link, and close predictably. No essential step belongs only in a hover card. An abbreviation such as MAE should expose its full name and interpretation, not simply expand into another unexplained technical phrase.

Definition content should distinguish overloaded words: model, parameter, weight, bias, validation, memory, agent, tool, skill, and confidence. “Bias” in a neuron, systematic forecast error, and unfair treatment are different concepts. “Validation” can refer to data/schema checks or model selection. The surrounding lesson must identify which meaning applies.

## Pacing, navigation, and cognitive load

Five days is a useful sequence, not evidence that every beginner will finish in 30 hours. Display separate reading, experiment, and practice budgets and retain the provisional timing label. Allow a learner to stop after a coherent section and return to the exact state. Technical details should deepen the main account rather than contain missing prerequisites that the main exercise secretly requires.

M09 should teach one common analytical pipeline with one required execution path; display parallel Excel/SQL/Power BI implementations as mapped alternatives. M12 needs staged disclosure around a persistent single token. M19 needs a persistent execution trace rather than a wall of infrastructure nouns. These are the three highest compression risks.

A novice should always be able to answer: What am I trying to do? What do I already know? Which control should I change? What result should I inspect? What can I conclude? What should I record? If the screen cannot support these questions, adding more prose elsewhere is unlikely to help.
